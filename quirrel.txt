In December of Harry’s fourth year, not long after the First Task of the Triwizard Tournament, Professor McGonagall announced that Hogwarts would be holding the Yule Ball on Christmas night. It was to be a formal dance for the students of Hogwarts, Beauxbatons, and Durmstrang, and it was immediately treated as one of the great events of the school year. Students in fourth year and above were allowed to attend, and younger students could go only if invited by an older one. What made the announcement much worse for Harry was that the Triwizard champions and their partners were expected to open the dancing. The moment he heard that, the Ball stopped sounding like a celebration and started sounding like a nightmare.

Almost at once, the castle changed. Girls began whispering together in corridors and giggling over who might ask whom, while the boys became tense, awkward, and secretive. Harry dreaded the whole business, because the person he really wanted to ask was Cho Chang. After a long struggle with his own nerves, he finally worked up the courage to ask her, only to find out that she had already agreed to go with Cedric Diggory. The answer left him crushed. It was bad enough that Cedric was another Hogwarts champion, admired by everyone and generally better at handling attention than Harry was; now he was also the one taking Cho to the Ball. Ron, meanwhile, made a fool of himself by asking Fleur Delacour in a sudden rush of courage that was really more panic than confidence. Fleur turned him down at once. Both boys were left without dates and with very little dignity.

At the same time, Hermione calmly revealed that she had already been asked by someone and had already said yes. Neither Harry nor Ron could quite believe it, and Ron seemed especially offended by the idea, though he had never thought of asking Hermione himself. Neville Longbottom also asked Hermione, but she had to refuse him for the same reason. Neville then turned to Ginny Weasley, who agreed to go with him. Harry and Ron delayed until they were nearly desperate, then finally asked Parvati Patil and Padma Patil. Harry went with Parvati, and Ron went with Padma. It was not a romantic triumph for either of them, only a last-minute escape from humiliation.

When Christmas night arrived, Harry and Ron had another problem to face: their dress robes. Harry’s were acceptable enough, but Ron’s were dreadful, old-fashioned maroon things trimmed with lace that looked more like something from a portrait than something a fourteen-year-old boy would willingly wear. Ron was furious and embarrassed before the Ball had even begun. Then the champions and their dates gathered to enter together, and Harry and Ron got the greatest shock of the evening. Hermione appeared on the arm of Viktor Krum. She looked so different that for a moment they hardly recognized her. Her hair was sleek, her robes were elegant, and she carried herself with such confidence that the whole room seemed forced to look at her. Ron stared at her in disbelief, and from that moment on, jealousy settled over him like a storm cloud.

The Great Hall had been transformed for the occasion. The long house tables had vanished and been replaced with many small, round tables lit by soft golden light. Everything glittered with winter magic. It was beautiful, but Harry had very little time to notice it, because he and the other champions had to lead the first dance. He dreaded every second of it. Being watched by the whole school while trying not to step on Parvati’s feet was exactly the sort of thing he hated. He felt stiff and awkward, and the only comfort was that once the champions had done their duty, the rest of the students joined in and the attention spread away from him.

Even then, Harry could not enjoy himself. His eyes kept straying to Cho, who was dancing with Cedric. Seeing them together soured his mood, and instead of making conversation or trying to enjoy Parvati’s company, he remained distracted and uncomfortable. Ron was even worse. Rather than paying attention to Padma, he spent much of the evening watching Hermione and Krum, growing more resentful every time he saw them together. Parvati and Padma had arrived expecting a proper evening at a grand dance, and neither of them was willing to spend it being ignored by miserable boys. Before long, they drifted away to dance and enjoy themselves with people who actually wanted to be there.

The Ball continued around them without much concern for Harry and Ron’s unhappiness. The Weird Sisters played, and the hall filled with music, dancing, and laughter. Many of the students had a splendid time. Fleur Delacour attended with Roger Davies, and Hagrid appeared in his best suit, clearly determined to impress Madame Maxime. He was delighted by her company and spent much of the evening with her. Later, away from the main crowd, Harry and Ron overheard a private conversation between them in which Hagrid, in a moment of trust and vulnerability, admitted that he was half-giant. He seemed to hope Madame Maxime would understand, perhaps because he believed she shared that heritage. Instead, she reacted with sharp denial and offended pride, refusing even the suggestion. The moment was painful and exposed just how much shame and prejudice surrounded giant blood.

Amid all the confusion of the evening, Cedric Diggory quietly did Harry a kindness. Remembering that Harry had warned him about the dragons before the First Task, Cedric repaid the favor by giving Harry a helpful hint about the golden egg. He did not explain the clue outright, but he nudged Harry in the right direction, suggesting that the egg would be easier to understand underwater. It was a generous act, and it showed that Cedric, for all Harry’s jealousy over Cho, was not arrogant or cruel.

The true climax of the Yule Ball came after the dancing, when Ron and Hermione had a furious argument. Ron, who had spent the whole evening smoldering over Hermione’s partnership with Krum, confronted her as if she had betrayed him. He acted possessive and angry, even though he had never asked her to the Ball and had shown no sign of wanting to go with her until he saw that someone else had claimed her first. Hermione, who had every reason to be furious, snapped back that Krum had asked her properly and that Ron had no right to behave as though she belonged to him. She pointed out that he had overlooked her until the last moment and that he was only angry because another boy had seen what he had failed to notice. The fight laid bare feelings that had been hidden under bickering for a long time. Ron’s jealousy made it obvious, even if he could not admit it honestly, that Hermione mattered to him far more than he had ever said.

By the end of the night, the Yule Ball had revealed much more than anyone’s choice of partner. Harry’s feelings for Cho had deepened into real hurt. Ron’s immaturity and jealousy had been dragged into the open. Hermione had shown that she could not be taken for granted, and that she was very capable of stepping outside the role the boys had unconsciously assigned her. Hagrid had exposed a painful secret of his own, and Cedric had shown unexpected decency. What was meant to be a glittering Christmas celebration became, for Harry and his friends, a night of embarrassment, longing, envy, and hard truths. Beneath the lights and music, the Ball forced them all a little closer to adulthood.
